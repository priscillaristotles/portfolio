Narrative Arc

A reusable seven-beat structure for building investor and sales narratives that compound — each beat earning the next. Shown applied to an anonymized early-stage SaaS pitch.

7 beats from tension to ask Reusable for pitch & sales decks Mapped to an emotional curve
01 The Arc

Seven beats. Each has a job in the larger story, and each sets up the one after it. The italic line is the beat's purpose (the reusable template); the box below is the anonymized applied example.

1
The Tension Open the loop
Name a problem the audience feels but hasn't articulated. Not features — friction. Make them nod before you've pitched anything.
Applied
Studio owners think their problem is scheduling. Reframe: their real problem is demand, conversion, and admin drowning — every hour on admin is an hour not filling classes.
2
The Shift Reframe the category
Show why the old way of thinking is obsolete. Move the audience from the category they know to the one you're defining.
Applied
Yesterday: booking software that manages what already exists. Today: growth infrastructure that also creates demand. The ground has moved.
3
The Solution Reveal the product
Now — and only now — introduce what you've built. The audience is primed to see it as the inevitable answer to the tension.
Applied
One platform, four layers: branded site · booking · payments · growth marketing. Everything to run and grow, without duct-taping five tools together.
4
The Unfair Advantage Establish the moat
The single thing competitors structurally cannot replicate. This is the beat that turns interest into belief.
Applied
Competitors give tools. We give tools + a live demand network of 200K+ active users already searching for activities. A front door, not just a back office.
5
The Proof Make it real
Evidence the story is already true, not aspirational. Traction, a case study, a metric that can't be argued with.
Applied
A founder-operated studio chain scaled 1→7 branches across four cities in ~18 months, entirely on the platform. Plus organic GMV across the network — all without paid acquisition.
6
The Market Size the prize
Show the opportunity is big enough to matter. Who it's for, how many, and why now is the moment.
Applied
Built for boutique wellness & fitness studios that have outgrown chat-based booking — a fast-growing, underserved segment across the region, expanding faster than tooling has kept up.
7
The Ask Close the loop
A specific, confident ask that resolves the tension opened in beat 1. Make the next step obvious and low-friction.
Applied
Clear raise amount, tied to specific use (team + GTM), with the milestone it unlocks. For sales: a free studio audit — 20 minutes, real numbers, no slides.
02 Emotional Curve

A good narrative isn't flat — it moves the audience through tension and release. This is the same seven beats, mapped to the feeling they should produce.

Tension → Resolution
The audience should feel the problem before they feel the relief. Peak tension at beat 1, peak conviction at beat 5.
The principle: Beat 5 (Proof) is the emotional peak — it's where conviction crystallizes, so it carries the heaviest evidence. The Ask doesn't need to be the highest point; it needs to feel like the natural release of everything built before it. A pitch that peaks at the ask is begging. A pitch that peaks at proof is closing.
03 Operating Principles

Four rules that hold the architecture together — apply on any deck.

01
Tension before solution, always
Never introduce the product until the audience feels the problem. A solution to an unfelt problem is just a feature list.
02
Each beat earns the next
No beat should be skippable. If you can remove one without breaking the logic, it wasn't doing work — cut it.
03
One idea per beat
Each beat carries a single claim. The moment a slide makes two arguments, the audience holds neither.
04
The throughline repeats
One phrase (here: "run and grow") recurs across beats so the audience leaves able to retell the story in a sentence.

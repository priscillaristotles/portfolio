01 The Framework

Six layers, each answering one question. The left column is the reusable template; the right shows it applied to the case. Work top to bottom — each layer constrains the next.

Positioning Canvas — applied
Template
Applied example
Layer 1
Market frame
What market are we actually in — as the customer sees it?
Template prompt
"Customers think they're buying [X]. The real job is [Y]."
Applied
Studios think they're buying booking software. The real job is running and growing the business — software is only half of it.
Layer 2
Category definition
Are we competing in an existing category or defining a new one?
Template prompt
"Not another [old category]. A [new category] that does [expanded job]."
Applied
Not another booking tool. An operating layer — the infrastructure underneath an entire activity category, including demand generation.
Layer 3
Primary contrast
Who do we define ourselves against — and on what axis?
Template prompt
"Incumbents give you [X]. We give you [X] + [the missing thing]."
Applied
Incumbents give you tools to manage what exists. We give you tools plus a demand network — a front door, not just a back office.
Layer 4
Unfair advantage
What can we claim that competitors structurally cannot?
Template prompt
"Only we have [structural asset] because [reason competitors can't copy it]."
Applied
Only we have a live consumer demand network (200K+ active users) — competitors are pure B2B tools and can't bolt on demand.
Layer 5
Proof anchor
What evidence makes the position believable, not aspirational?
Template prompt
"[Concrete metric / case] proves the position is real today."
Applied
A founder-operated 7-branch studio chain scaled entirely on the platform — proof the operating layer works in the wild.
Layer 6
Message
The one line that compresses all five layers above.
Template prompt
"[Verb] the [thing]. [Verb] the [bigger thing]."
Applied
"Run the studio. Grow the business." — captures ops + growth in four words.
02 The Statement

When all six layers resolve, they compress into a single positioning statement — the north star every piece of messaging traces back to.

Positioning Statement · Applied Example
For boutique studios that have outgrown chat-based booking, the platform is the operating layer that runs operations and grows demand — because unlike booking tools, it comes with a live network of activity-seekers already looking to book.

Why this works: The axes aren't neutral — they're chosen so the product lands alone in the most valuable quadrant. A good positioning map isn't an objective survey of the market; it's an argument about which dimensions matter, framed so your position looks inevitable.
04 How to Reuse

The framework applies to any brand. Four steps to run it on a new engagement.

1
Fill layers 1–2
Interview the customer. Find the gap between what they think they buy and the real job.
2
Map the contrast
Identify the incumbent and the single axis where you win. Build the 2×2.
3
Anchor with proof
Find the one piece of evidence that makes the claim believable today, not someday.
4
Compress to message
Distill all layers into one line a stranger repeats correctly after hearing it once.

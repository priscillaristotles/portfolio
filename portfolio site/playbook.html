01 Approach

The approach

Two structural choices that make the plan disciplined and easy to fund.

1. Phases are gated by outcomes, not months. Instead of "Phase 2 starts in August," it's "Phase 2 unlocks when we hit 10 paid studios." This means we don't waste budget scaling channels before they've proven themselves, and we don't get trapped pacing to a calendar instead of pacing to reality. If we hit 10 paid studios in week 6, we accelerate. If it takes 12 weeks, we stay lean longer.

2. Budget is deliberately lean — $12K–$17K total over 12 months. We start at $200–$330/month for the first 3 months when we're still learning what converts. Only after proof do we scale spend — and even then, in measured steps tied to milestones.

The pitch to the CEO

"I want to be conservative on spend until we have data. Give me $200–$330/month for the first 3 months — that's enough for tools, light content, and a small ads test. When I hit 10 paid studios, that's the proof we need: it means the playbook works, and I'll come back with a request to scale to $660/month. From there, every spend increase is tied to a concrete milestone I've already delivered."

The 25/month average is misleading. The real growth curve looks more like: 5/month in months 1–3, 15/month in months 4–6, 30/month in months 7–9, 35/month in months 10–12. This compounding pattern is why outcome gating works — we earn the right to scale by proving each phase.

Why the budget is small and still works

We're not scaling all six channels simultaneously. In the first 3 months, only direct outreach matters — we don't need $2.3K/month for paid ads when we don't yet know which ad creative converts. The phased approach means we pay for what we've proven works, not for hypothetical scale.

The "behind schedule" rule

If we haven't hit a gate milestone by the suggested time window (e.g. still at 7 paid after 4 months), we don't automatically advance the budget. We diagnose first: is the bottleneck product, message, or sourcing? Stay in the current phase, fix the bottleneck, then advance. This protects against the most common startup failure mode — scaling spend on top of a broken funnel.

Total 12-month budget

$12K–$17K across 12 months (~$1K–$1.5K/month average). At 300 paid studios × $40 MRR = ~$12K new monthly recurring revenue. The full marketing budget pays itself back in 1.3 months of run-rate MRR at the end of the year. Blended CAC across all channels: ~~$53 per acquired studio — strong for Southeast Asian B2B SaaS.

Budget guardrails — promise to the CEO

(1) No automatic budget release between phases. Each gate requires a fresh approval based on hard milestones. (2) Monthly CAC review per channel. Any channel with CAC >2x target after 2 months → paused and reallocated. (3) Hard ceiling per channel. Never more than 40% of monthly spend on any single platform. (4) No vanity events. Every event tracked: attendees, conversions, attributed revenue.

12 The call

Discovery call framework

20 minutes. No slides on first call. Listen more than you talk.

20-minute structure
Min 0–2
Context set. "Aku gak mau jualan apapun hari ini — aku cuma mau ngerti situasi kalian dulu."
Min 2–8
Q1: Current booking process. Walk me through DM → check-in. Don't interrupt.
Min 8–13
Q2: New member acquisition. Where do they come from? Reveals bottleneck type.
Min 13–17
Q3: Admin time cost. Hours/week on manual booking? Quantify the pain.
Min 17–20
Value match + next step. Match what you heard. Propose trial. Never leave without next action.
Two bottleneck types — pitch accordingly

Bottleneck = admin ops: Lead with booking + scheduling + payment automation. "Save X jam/minggu yang bisa dipakai isi kelas."
Bottleneck = growth: Lead with discovery via the platform's 200K monthly active users on consumer side. "Member baru datang tanpa bayar ads."

Every objection is information. Engage directly — don't deflect.

Mahal. Member kita belum banyak.

Sistemnya stabil belum?
Valid — dan aku gak over-promise. Yang udah proven: booking, payment, scheduling, member management, dipake 1,800+ providers termasuk founder's studio network yang aku founder-nya sendiri. Yang masih kita improve: [be specific]. Kalau itu kritis buat kalian, kita bicarain timeline-nya jujur.

17 Risks

Risks & mitigations

Five things most likely to derail this plan. Plan for each before it happens.

  • Risk 1 — Product instability burns early customers. If onboarding fails for first 5–10 paid, word kills the playbook. Mitigation: Map exact gaps with CEO before Gate 1 starts. Refuse to sell unstable features.
  • Risk 2 — Burnout in Phase 2. Solo CBO running everything. Mitigation: Plan a part-time BD or intern by end of Phase 2 / start of Phase 3, before you're overloaded.
  • Risk 3 — Ads don't convert. If Meta CAC stays >~$53 after 2 months of optimization, channel is broken. Mitigation: Built-in kill switch — pause and reallocate to events.
  • Risk 4 — founder's studio network operational drag. Day job creep eats sales time. Mitigation: Strict time-boxing. Delegate founder's studio network ops to branch managers.
  • Risk 5 — Series A fundraising pulls you off sales. When [lead investor] gets deep, you're in deck mode. Mitigation: Build pipeline ahead of fundraising. Get the engine running before diligence.
Honest summary for the CEO

300 studios in 12 months with one CBO and $12K–$17K is tight but achievable. The plan only works if: (1) product holds up under early customer load, (2) we hit each gate before scaling spend, (3) we hire support by end of Phase 2 if pace requires. The outcome-gated structure is the protection — we don't waste money on hypothetical scale. Build to hit 300. Be okay landing at 220 if the curve is slower than projected.
